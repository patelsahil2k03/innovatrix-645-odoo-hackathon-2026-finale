Innovatrix #645 Odoo Hackathon 2026 — Final Round Problem-statement selection

DealFlow or Ledger

Two of the three problem statements look similar on the surface. They are not. Here is what actually differs, what each domain model costs to build, and what gets cut when the clock runs out.

The question on the table

“There’s a lot of overlap” — half true, and the half that’s false is the half that matters

The overlap is real, but it sits entirely on the perimeter: five shared concepts — Customer, Product, Order, Invoice, Payment. Both statements walk the same outer path of master data → document → payment → report.

The cores are disjoint. Furniture’s centre of gravity is the general ledger: the interesting object is a balanced journal entry, and the hard problem is an accounting invariant. DealFlow’s centre of gravity is the quotation state machine: the interesting object is an approval chain, and the hard problem is orchestration. Neither core appears anywhere in the other statement.

Counted as domain concepts, the shared perimeter is 42% of Furniture and only 21% of DealFlow — which is another way of saying DealFlow is roughly twice the build.

Counted at concept level, not table level, to avoid false precision — one concept such as “Sales Order” is two tables (header and lines). Table counts land at roughly 19 for Furniture and 26 for DealFlow.

The consequence worth acting on

DealFlow360 has no ledger. Furniture is nothing but ledger. DealFlow stops at “invoice status updates correctly” — there is no chart of accounts, no double entry, no P&L anywhere in its 13 pages.

So Furniture’s entire core is precisely DealFlow’s missing back end. If we build DealFlow and bolt a minimal posting engine underneath it, we hold a differentiator that no other DealFlow team will have — harvested from the statement we didn’t pick. That is the strongest argument for treating these as one decision rather than two.

The one algorithm to get exactly right

§10 spells this out with a worked example, which means it can be implemented to the letter and defended line by line. It must handle both stated cases: one badly-over line, and many slightly-over lines that individually look fine.

for each line: ceiling = min(customer_tier_ceiling, product_category_ceiling) overage = max(0, discount_pct − ceiling) weight = line_subtotal ÷ order_subtotal blended_score = Σ (overage × weight) ← many small violations worst_line = max(overage) ← one large violation route_on = max(blended_score, worst_line) ↳ matched against approval_rules → ordered list of required roles

Worked from the statement’s own example: a Gold customer at a 15% tier ceiling, with a Service line discounted 18% against a 10% category ceiling, is 8 points over on that line — so the whole quotation routes for approval even though 15% “sounds fine on paper”.

Domain model — Option B

Urban Furniture

The statement is six pages and mentions double entry almost in passing. That thinness is the opportunity: most teams will store balances on invoices and sum them into a balance sheet.

Master data7 tables
contactsproducts accountsjournals analytic_accountsbudgets budget_lines

Contacts and products carry account mappings — receivable_account_id, income_account_id — which is what lets a document post itself without the user choosing accounts by hand.

The ledger — the core2 tables
journal_entriesjournal_lines

Two tables carry the entire differentiator. journal_entries holds a gapless sequence number, an immutable POSTED state and a reversed_by_id; corrections create a reversing entry rather than an update. The Σ debit = Σ credit rule belongs in a database constraint, not only in application code.

Documents — each one posts to the ledger10 tables
purchase_orderspurchase_order_lines vendor_billsvendor_bill_lines sales_orderssales_order_lines customer_invoicescustomer_invoice_lines paymentspayment_allocations

Furniture is bidirectional — it has a purchase side (PO → Bill) that DealFlow has no equivalent of. payment_allocations is what allows one payment to settle several invoices partially, which the statement doesn’t ask for and every real system needs.

The one invariant to get exactly right

post(document) → exactly one balanced journal_entry constraint: Σ debit = Σ credit Balance Sheet = Σ journal_lines group by account.type ∈ {ASSET, LIABILITY, EQUITY} P&L = Σ journal_lines group by account.type ∈ {INCOME, EXPENSE} Trial balance = Σ debit − Σ credit ≡ 0 ← assert live, on screen correction = reversing entry, never UPDATE retry = idempotency_key, never a second posting

Reports aggregate the ledger and never sum documents. That single rule is the difference between an accounting system and an invoice list with a balance sheet drawn on top — and it is visible from the outside the moment a judge asks what happens after a credit note.

The case for each

Why take it

ArgumentDealFlow360Urban Furniture
Scored criterion #1 — live data Native. Deal health, stalled alerts, live margin, stock moving under a split. Engineered. The ledger has to be made to visibly move.
Reuses what we already built Most. §A3 requires approvals logged with user, timestamp and reason — that is our audit middleware, already tested. Some. Audit is useful here but not required by the statement.
Specification quality 13 pages, an algorithm with a worked example, and an 8-step test flow that is effectively the judges’ script. 6 pages. Thin enough that meeting it is easy and exceeding it is obvious.
Differentiation ceiling High — genuinely implementing the hard parts instead of faking them. Highest relative to the statement — a real posting engine beats an invoice list outright.
Confidence of finishing Lower. Twice the domain model. Highest. Smallest, simplest model of the three statements.
Demo drama A quote routes itself to Finance and explains why; the customer counters from a portal and it re-routes live. A number on a report opens the journal line that produced it.
Second UI surface A genuinely separate customer portal — required to be real, and few teams will build one. A smaller contact portal — view own invoices, pay.
Where the recommendation could be wrong

DealFlow is roughly twice the build, and the argument that our foundation absorbs the difference is a judgement, not a measurement. If the ranking is wrong, this is where it breaks — and the failure shows up late, around the point where the portal and the subscription billing both still need writing.

Furniture is the safer pick and I won’t dress that up as a weakness. It is also plausibly the more crowded pick, precisely because it is safer — but that is unknowable from here, and I’m not going to guess at what other teams will choose.

Before this is decided

Three open items

01

Open the mockup links. Both PDFs end with an Excalidraw URL showing the UI the evaluator has in mind. Nobody has opened them yet, and they could change the effort estimate materially in either direction.

02

Decide whether the ledger rides along. If DealFlow is chosen, adding a minimal posting engine underneath it is the single strongest differentiator available — and also the fastest way to overrun. It is a deliberate call, not something to drift into at hour eighteen.

03

Pick the one wow, and only one. Both cut lists name a single item. Two wow features is how a team ends up with neither finished.
