Innovatrix #645 Urban Furniture — Accounting System

Walnut Design System

An accounting interface should feel precise and trustworthy, not airy and marketed. Everything here is dense, quiet, and built so the numbers are the loudest thing on the screen.

Direction

Why walnut, and why nothing else is coloured

The accent is furniture, not software

Walnut is a material this business actually works in. It is also almost unused as a software accent — the default reach is a blue or a violet, and either would have made this look like every other dashboard. Warm neutrals are biased toward it so the greys read as chosen rather than inherited.

Semantic colour is held completely separate

Green, amber and red mean state — paid, partial, over budget. They are never the brand. If the accent were also green, an over-budget figure would stop reading as a warning, which is the one thing that colour has to do here.

One rule for the whole palette

Re-theming happens by changing --accent and the neutral bias. Nothing in the semantic set moves — those five colours mean the same thing in every theme, in both light and dark, and in print.

Type

Two faces, one for reading and one for counting

Schibsted Grotesk for everything read as language. IBM Plex Mono for everything read as a value — money, dates, document numbers, account codes. The split is not decorative: digits must line up in columns, and only the mono face guarantees it.

Money has house rules, and they are not negotiable

Always two decimals · always grouped in the Indian system (₹1,24,500.00, not ₹124,500.00) · always right-aligned · always tabular-nums · never computed in JavaScript. Totals, tax and balances arrive from the server already correct. Floating-point drift in a browser is how a demo shows ₹999.9999999.

Rules

What keeps this from drifting

Locked in hour one

  • Every colour and space comes from a token. No hex in a component, no arbitrary pixel values.
  • The scale is 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64. Nothing between.
  • Layout uses flex or grid with gap — never per-element margins that collapse.
  • Wide tables scroll inside their own container. The page body never scrolls sideways.

Accessibility that is easy to lose

  • Status is never colour alone — a dot and a word travel with it.
  • Sortable headers carry aria-sort; the arrow glyph is sighted-only.
  • Focus is always visible, and trapped inside an open dialog.
  • Tap targets stay ≥ 40px at 360 wide.

Print is a real surface here, not an afterthought

Invoices, bills and reports are printed and emailed as PDF, so a print stylesheet is part of the design rather than a nicety: drop the shell and navigation, force the ground to white and the ink to black, keep table rules, and never let a document table break a row across pages. Semantic colour must survive greyscale — which it does, because status always carries a word.
