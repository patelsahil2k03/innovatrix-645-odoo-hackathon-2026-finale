Innovatrix #645 Urban Furniture — Accounting System System design

Ledger Architecture

Every document in this system posts into one ledger through one function. That single constraint is what makes the reports trustworthy — and it is the thing most easily lost at three in the morning.

The shape

Two front ends, one API, one ledger

The constraint the whole design rests on

Documents never store a balance that a report reads. A document posts; a report aggregates. Break that and the balance sheet becomes decoration — it will still render, still look right, and be wrong the moment anything is cancelled.

The mechanism

Four document types, one door into the ledger

This is the diagram worth memorising. Every arrow into journal_lines passes through one function — and the crossed path is the one that will be attempted.

Why a duplicate is worse than an error here

Two concurrent posts of the same invoice produce two balanced entries. The trial balance still reads zero, every report still renders, and the books are silently doubled. That is why the row lock comes before the status re-check, and why UNIQUE(source_type, source_id) exists as a backstop at the database.

Sequence

What happens when a document is confirmed

01

Router — authenticate, check the role. require_roles("Admin","Accountant"). No business logic lives here.

02

Lock the row — lock_row(db, CustomerInvoice, id) takes SELECT … FOR UPDATE. On SQLite this clause is silently dropped, which is why anything graded runs on Postgres.

03

Re-check status after the lock — require_status(inv.status, DRAFT). A check made before the lock is a check against a value that may already be stale.

04

Build the lines from the data, never from constants: receivable off the contact, income off the product, tax off the line. A missing mapping raises MISSING_ACCOUNT_MAPPING rather than falling back to a default account.

05

Post — balance check, duplicate guard, sequence number allocated under its own lock, insert.

06

Commit. Then publish document.posted and ledger.changed. An event published before the commit can describe a transaction that then rolls back.

Corrections reverse, never mutate

Cancelling a posted document writes a second entry with debit and credit swapped and reversal_of_id set. Two immutable entries now exist and the trial balance still lands on zero.

This is also the direct answer to “what happens after a credit note?” — nothing is deleted, a second entry records the correction.

Payments are idempotent

A double-clicked Pay button would otherwise produce two payments, two balanced entries, and books that are quietly wrong. A unique idempotency_key returns the original payment on a retry — a retry is not an error.

Derived data

How a budget knows what it has achieved

The one computation that does not read the ledger — and the reason it doesn't is worth understanding before you build it.

Budgets have their own state machine

DRAFT → CONFIRMED → REVISED → CANCELLED. Revising a confirmed budget creates a new budget, moves the original to REVISED, appends “Revised” to the name, and links the two in both directions. The original is never edited — the same immutability instinct as the ledger, applied to planning.
