Urban Furniture · Accounting System

The full story of a purchase and a sale, in journal entries.

Follow one week in the life of Urban Furniture. Watch how buying chairs from Azure and selling them to Nimesh becomes four balanced journal entries, and how those entries roll up into a Balance Sheet that proves itself.

Business: Urban Furniture Period: March to April 2026 Method: Double-entry, accrual basis
CAST

Who is involved.

Four actors participate in this story. Only two of them ever log into Urban Furniture's system. The other two exist in the system as data.

Urban Furniture Admin
Sets up master data, creates all transactions, sees all reports.
Invoicing User
Records daily transactions and payments. No delete rights.
Azure Furniture
Vendor. Exists only as a Contact record. Never logs in.
Nimesh Pathak
Customer. Optional Contact Portal login to see own invoices.

PRINCIPLE

Why two entries per flow.

Each flow creates two journal entries because two distinct real-world events happen at different moments. The bill or invoice records an obligation. The payment records the settlement. Combining them would hide who owes what during the credit period.

Account type Debit effect Credit effect
Assets (Cash, Bank, Debtors, Input Tax) Increases Decreases
Liabilities (Creditors, Output Tax, Loans) Decreases Increases
Equity (Capital, Retained Earnings) Decreases Increases
Income (Sales) Decreases Increases
Expenses (Purchase, Rent, Salary) Increases Decreases

RECAP

What the system does, in one glance.

Master data is set up once. Documents are created by users. The system silently posts the accounting behind each validated document. Reports read live from the ledger.

Purchase (buying)

1. Purchase Order
Plan to buy. No accounting.
2. Vendor Bill
Records obligation. Debit expense and tax, credit creditors.
3. Payment
Records settlement. Debit creditors, credit bank.

Sales (selling)

1. Sales Order
Plan to sell. No accounting.
2. Customer Invoice
Records receivable. Debit debtors, credit income and tax.
3. Payment
Records settlement. Debit bank, credit debtors.
